VoxScribe · design directions · round two

Four rooms, rebuilt from the floor

Round-one feedback: too close to the current app — same skeleton, new paint. So this round changes the furniture, not just the walls: each direction rethinks the main window's layout, and each shows its pill in the three states you actually see — recording, working, done. Bench Instrument is dropped. The red lamp stays red everywhere.

DIRECTION A

Signal House

A rack unit, not a window. The transport moves to the bottom deck like a tape machine: oversized record key, a real needle VU meter, a glowing counter, hardware UNDO key. Dictations become numbered takes on a tape log. No accent colour — lamps and cream ink carry all state; the red jewel is the only saturated object.

DIRECTION B

The Manuscript

The window disappears; a sheet of paper on a desk remains. No icon rail, no cards — navigation is a letterhead, dictations flow as a day-by-day journal with times in the margin, dictionary corrections shown as amber underlines. The record control is a dark wax seal riding the page's corner. The pill is a strip of the same paper with a red seal-dot while recording.

DIRECTION C

The Ticker

Sound is the subject, so sound is the interface. The main window is an audio timeline: each dictation is a clip with its own waveform, laid on hour lanes like a session in an audio editor, with a scrubber that doubles as the day's map. And the pill stops being a pill — it becomes a full-width ticker hugging the bottom edge of the screen, the waveform streaming through it while you speak, the transcript chasing it. Violet, not cyan.

Palette
TypeSegoe UI + Consolas; the waveforms are the display face.
CostHighest. Timeline view, waveform thumbnails per transcript, new ticker overlay window.

DIRECTION D

Deep Field

The dark room kept, but recomposed. The rail and the voice band are gone; a hero instrument panel owns the top of the window — a huge counter, today's stats, the live waveform stretched wide — and transcripts hang under it as a bare, borderless list separated by hairlines. Opaque surfaces, steel-cyan, no translucency anywhere except the pill itself, which keeps its glass as the one artefact of the old identity.

Recommendation, round two

Signal House is now the pick for personality; Deep Field for pragmatism. A's bottom transport deck and take-log are a genuine re-architecture that no dictation app on the market looks like, and the needle VU meter gives the app the physical mass the current glass lacks. Deep Field gets 70% of the solidity for a fraction of the work. The Ticker is the wild card — the full-width bar is the most original overlay concept of the four, and it could be adopted by any of the other directions even if its timeline window never ships.

All three keep red = recording, keep amber for instrumentation, and keep the overlay non-focusable — the doctrine survives every direction except where a plate says it doesn't.
